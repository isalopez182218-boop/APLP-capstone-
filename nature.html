Inchworm head

An inchworm moves in two holds. It grips with its back legs and stretches forward, then grips with its front legs and pulls the back end up behind it. The inchworm head walks along the cable the same way.

Clamp A sits on the cutting head, just above the cut. Clamp B rides two rods on a 40 mm lead screw. While B holds the cable, the screw pulls the head forward 40 mm and the burr cuts one band. Then A grips, B lets go and steps 40 mm ahead. Four steps strip 6 in, and both clamps always hold uncut insulation.

The screw sets every step, so the head never loses its place on the cable, and the whole unit is about 180–200 mm long instead of 316 mm. This is an idea for the next revision. The November prototype still uses two fixed end clamps.

The same walk can be driven by a single actuator. This video shows the mechanism.
