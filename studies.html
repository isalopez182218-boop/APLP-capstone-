15Studies and schedules

Eleven machine concepts were scored against eleven criteria under two sets of weights: one for the final product and one for the 17 November prototype. The two answers differ. For the final product, a short head that walks along the cable (concepts G, J and I) beats a long carriage, but the three walking heads are within the scoring noise of each other. For the prototype, the most mature design, v4 (F), leads. Bench tests, not more scoring, should pick between G, J and I.

Draft 2, 3 November 2026. Scores are the design team’s first-pass estimates and have not yet been reviewed by the sponsor. Download the workbook (Excel, live formulas: change a weight or a score and the totals update).

Method

Each concept gets a score from 1 to 5 on each criterion, using the written guide in Table 1 so that a score means the same thing for every concept. The weighted score is the sum of weight × score divided by 5, so a concept scoring 5 everywhere gets 100.

The final-product weights follow the sponsor’s stated priority (size, then weight, then power) and the hard requirements in the statement of work. The prototype weights favour what can be built and proven by 17 November, so technical risk and manufacturability carry the most weight. Equal weights are shown as a check that the result is not an artefact of the weights.

Three limits apply. The scores are estimates from CAD checks, hand calculations and the 15 October specimen trials, not measurements of built machines. Differences under about 3 points are inside the noise. Several concepts were developed by the same people who scored them; to keep the comparison fair, concepts that share a subsystem get the same score for it (for example, every concept with the follower depth wheel gets the same depth score).

  1. Walking heads win the final product. G, J and I all drop the 6 in feed stroke, so they are short enough for the 12 in envelope and support the cable close to the cut. Their scores (78.0, 75.0 and 74.4) are 3 to 4 points apart, which is about the size of the scoring noise.
  2. v4 (F) is the right prototype. It leads on prototype weights (73.2) because it is CAD-checked and printable. Its weakness for the final product is length: about 316 mm against the 305 mm (12 in) limit.
  3. The order at the top holds under equal weights. G (74.5) and J (72.7) still lead; F, H and I tie at 70.9 just behind. The conclusion does not depend on the chosen weights, but the gap between I and the inchworms narrows.
  4. Two bench tests can change the order. If the wheel crawler (I) passes a traction and position test (axial feed 2 → 4, risk 2 → 3), it scores 79.2 and leads. If the inside-clamping inchworm (G) passes its re-grip test (risk 2 → 3), it scores 79.6. Both tests are cheap and should run before the final design is frozen.
  5. C and D stay out under every weight set. C is too heavy and does not close to 3/4 in cable; D needs about 1,274 N of thrust against roughly 630 N of roller traction.
